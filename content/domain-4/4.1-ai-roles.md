### Why named roles matter
- An AI system passes through many hands: data collection, training, packaging, deployment, operation, review
  - Each handoff is a place where a control can be skipped because "someone else checks that"
- Named roles give every control an owner
  - Who classifies the training data? Who signs the model? Who can approve production?
- ==Separation of duties== applies to models the way it applies to code and money
  - The person who trains a model does not approve its release
  - The person who runs a model does not audit it
- Titles vary; one person may hold several roles in a small shop, so match the task in a question to the role that owns it

### Data and model builders
- These roles create the model; each owns a share of its security

:::wide
| Role | Owns | Security responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| ==Data engineer== | Pipelines that collect, clean, label, and deliver data; feature stores; RAG ingestion jobs | Data provenance and lineage, classification at ingest, poisoning and quality checks, access control on data stores |
| ==Data scientist== | Exploring data, choosing features and algorithms, training and evaluating models | Keep sensitive data out of notebooks and experiments, prevent train/test leakage, measure bias, document datasets and results |
| ==Machine learning engineer== | Turning an experimental model into production code: fine-tuning, optimization, serving, application integration | Safe model formats (`safetensors` over pickle), pinned dependencies, input validation and output handling around the model |
:::

- Data engineer moves and prepares data; data scientist analyzes it and builds the model
- Data scientist proves the model works; ML engineer makes it run reliably inside a product

### Architecture and operations roles
- These roles decide how the model fits the environment and keep it running

:::wide
| Role | Owns | Security responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| ==AI architect== | End-to-end solution design: model choice, RAG or fine-tune, how the model connects to apps, data, and tools | Trust boundaries, least-privilege tool access for agents, where guardrails and the gateway sit |
| ==Platform engineer== | Shared infrastructure: GPU clusters, Kubernetes, model gateway, vector databases, self-service environments | Tenant isolation, secrets management, network segmentation, patching, quotas and cost limits |
| ==MLOps engineer== | CI/CD for models: training pipelines, model registry, versioning, deployment, drift monitoring, rollback | Signed artifacts, registry permissions, approval gates in the pipeline, provenance records (ML-BOM), alerting on drift |
:::

- Overlaps the exam likes to probe
  - ML engineer makes one model production-ready; MLOps engineer automates the path every model takes
  - Platform engineer runs the infrastructure all teams share; AI architect designs one solution on top of it

### Security, governance, and assurance roles
- These roles do not ship the model; they decide whether it is safe enough to ship and prove that it was

:::wide
| Role | Owns | Security responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| ==AI security architect== | The security reference architecture for AI; threat models using OWASP LLM Top 10 and MITRE ATLAS; red-team scope | Required controls per risk tier: prompt filtering, output handling, agent permissions, logging, model supply chain checks |
| ==AI governance engineer== | Governance as code: policy checks in pipelines, the AI inventory, model cards, automated evidence collection | Turns policy into gates a pipeline cannot skip; keeps audit evidence complete and current |
| ==AI risk analyst== | Identifying and rating AI risks; impact assessments; the AI risk register; reporting to the governance committee | Rates likelihood and impact, assigns a risk tier, tracks treatment and residual risk to sign-off |
| ==AI auditor== | Independent review of AI systems and the governance program against policy, law, and standards (ISO/IEC 42001, EU AI Act) | Tests that controls exist and work, samples evidence, reports findings; must be independent of build and run teams |
:::

- Governance engineer versus risk analyst
  - Governance engineer builds the machinery: the gate in the pipeline, the inventory sync, the evidence export
  - Risk analyst makes the judgment: how bad, how likely, which tier, is the residual risk acceptable
- Auditor versus everyone else
  - The auditor gives assurance to the board or a regulator
  - An auditor who helped design the control cannot objectively test it

### A RACI for one scenario
- ==RACI==: Responsible (does the work), Accountable (owns the outcome; exactly one per task), Consulted (gives input before), Informed (told after)
- Scenario: a SOC team fine-tunes a small model to summarize alerts, and it is going to production behind the company's model gateway

:::wide
| Task | Responsible | Accountable | Consulted / Informed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Approve the training data sources | Data engineer | Data owner (SOC manager) | C: AI risk analyst, privacy; I: data scientist |
| Fine-tune and evaluate the model | Data scientist | ML engineering lead | C: AI security architect; I: MLOps engineer |
| Red-team and bias testing | AI security architect | CISO | C: data scientist; I: AI risk analyst |
| Rate the risk and set the tier | AI risk analyst | Governance committee chair | C: AI security architect, legal; I: CoE |
| Sign, register, and gate the model | MLOps engineer | Platform owner | C: AI governance engineer; I: SOC manager |
| Deploy behind the gateway | Platform engineer | Platform owner | C: AI architect; I: SOC analysts |
| Approve go-live | Governance committee | Governance committee chair | C: CISO, data owner; I: internal audit |
| Monitor drift, abuse, and cost | MLOps engineer | SOC manager | C: AI security architect; I: AI risk analyst |
| Audit the control evidence | AI auditor | Head of internal audit | C: AI governance engineer; I: board |
:::

- Separation of duties shows in the A column: the data scientist who tuned the model is never accountable for go-live, and the auditor reports outside the build chain
- Every row has exactly one A; two accountable people means nobody is

:::try
- Take an AI system you know (a help desk chatbot, a RAG search over your policy library)
- List five tasks from data intake to retirement and fill in R and A for each
- Circle any row where the same person is both R and A for an approval; decide who should take the A
:::

:::exam
- Match the task to the role: building data pipelines is the data engineer; automating model deployment and rollback is the MLOps engineer; running GPU clusters and the gateway is the platform engineer.
- "Independent assessment of whether AI controls meet ISO/IEC 42001" is the AI auditor. A distractor will offer the AI governance engineer, who builds the controls.
- Rating and tracking AI risk is the AI risk analyst; designing the security controls that treat it is the AI security architect.
:::
