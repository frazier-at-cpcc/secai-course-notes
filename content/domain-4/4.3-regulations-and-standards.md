### Laws, frameworks, and standards
- Law is ==mandatory==, with regulators and fines (the EU AI Act)
- Principles and frameworks are ==voluntary== guidance (OECD AI Principles, NIST AI RMF)
- Standards are auditable requirements, some ==certifiable== (ISO/IEC 42001)
- Compliance reaches the whole life cycle: what you may build, what must be tested and documented, what users must be told

### European Union (EU) AI Act
- ==European Union (EU) AI Act==: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the first comprehensive AI law
  - Entered into force 1 August 2024; obligations phase in over several years
  - Amended by the ==AI Omnibus==, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (in force 27 July 2026), which pushed back the high-risk dates
- Reaches beyond Europe: covers non-EU providers whose AI is sold in the EU or whose output is used there
- ==Risk-based==: obligations scale with the harm a use can cause

:::figure 4.3-eu-risk-tiers.svg | The four EU AI Act risk tiers. General-purpose AI models carry a separate set of obligations alongside the tiers.

- Unacceptable risk: banned outright (Article 5)
  - Harmful manipulation; exploiting age or disability; social scoring; predicting crime from profiling alone
  - Untargeted facial-image scraping; emotion recognition at work or school; inferring sensitive traits from biometrics
  - Real-time remote biometric ID in public for policing (narrow exceptions)
  - Added by the AI Omnibus: AI that generates non-consensual intimate imagery or child sexual abuse material
- ==High risk==: allowed with strict duties
  - Safety components of products under EU product laws (Annex I: machinery, toys, medical devices)
  - Annex III uses: biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, essential services such as credit scoring, law enforcement, migration and border control, justice and democratic processes
  - Provider duties: risk management system, data governance, technical documentation, logging, human oversight, accuracy and cybersecurity, quality management, conformity assessment, CE marking, EU database registration
- Limited risk: transparency duties (Article 50)
  - Tell people they are dealing with a chatbot; label deepfakes; mark generated content in a machine-readable way
- Minimal risk: spam filters, game AI; no new obligations
- ==General-purpose AI (GPAI) models==: documentation, copyright policy, and a training-content summary
  - Models trained with more than 10<sup>25</sup> FLOPs are presumed to carry systemic risk and must also evaluate, red-team, report incidents, and secure the model

### Roles, dates, and penalties
- Roles: the Act calls all of them ==operators==

| Role | Who |
|---|---|
| ==Provider== | Develops an AI system or GPAI model and places it on the market under its own name |
| ==Deployer== | Uses an AI system under its authority in a professional context |
| Importer | EU-based; brings in a system bearing a non-EU provider's name |
| Distributor | Makes it available in the supply chain; neither provider nor importer |
| Authorized representative | EU-based; holds a written mandate from a non-EU provider |

- Most obligations sit with the provider; deployers of high-risk systems must follow the instructions, assign human oversight, monitor, and keep the logs
- A deployer becomes a provider (Article 25) if it puts its own name on a high-risk system, substantially modifies it, or changes its purpose so that it becomes high-risk
  - Fine-tuning a vendor model and shipping it under your brand is a common way to cross that line

:::figure wide 4.3-eu-timeline.svg | EU AI Act application dates as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. The high-risk rules moved from August 2026 to December 2027 and August 2028.

- GPAI models already on the market before 2 August 2025 have until 2 August 2027 to comply
- ==Penalty ceilings== (Article 99): whichever is higher, a fixed sum or a share of worldwide annual turnover

| Violation | Up to |
|---|---|
| Prohibited practice (Article 5) | EUR 35 million or 7% |
| Most other obligations | EUR 15 million or 3% |
| Incorrect or misleading information to authorities | EUR 7.5 million or 1% |
| GPAI model providers (Article 101) | EUR 15 million or 3% |

- For SMEs and start-ups the lower of the two amounts applies

### OECD AI Principles
- ==Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) standards==: the OECD AI Principles, the first intergovernmental AI standard
  - Adopted May 2019; updated May 2024; 47 adherents
  - Voluntary, but they shaped the G20 AI principles and the EU AI Act, whose definition of an "AI system" closely follows the OECD's
- Five values-based principles for AI actors
  - Inclusive growth, sustainable development and well-being
  - Human rights and democratic values, including fairness and privacy
  - Transparency and explainability
  - Robustness, security and safety
  - Accountability
- Five recommendations for governments: invest in AI R&D, build an inclusive AI ecosystem, interoperable governance and policy, human capacity and labor market transition, international cooperation
- The ==2024 update== responded to general-purpose and generative AI
  - Safeguards to override, repair, or decommission a system that behaves in unwanted ways
  - Misinformation and information integrity; respect for intellectual property
  - Responsible business conduct across the life cycle; clearer transparency disclosures; environmental sustainability

### ISO/IEC AI standards
- ==International Organization for Standardization (ISO) AI standards== are joint ISO/IEC work from committee JTC 1/SC 42

:::wide
| Standard | Title (short) | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| ==ISO/IEC 42001:2023== | AI management system (AIMS) | Certifiable requirements for running an AI program; the AI counterpart to ISO/IEC 27001 |
| ISO/IEC 23894:2023 | Guidance on AI risk management | Applying ISO 31000 risk management to AI |
| ISO/IEC 22989:2022 | AI concepts and terminology | Shared vocabulary for contracts, policies, and other standards |
| ISO/IEC 23053:2022 | Framework for AI systems using ML | Reference description of an ML system's components |
| ISO/IEC 42005:2025 | AI system impact assessment | Assessing effects on people and society |
| ISO/IEC 42006:2025 | Requirements for AIMS audit and certification bodies | Who may certify you against 42001 |
| ISO/IEC 38507:2022 | Governance implications of AI use | Board and executive oversight of AI |
| ISO/IEC TR 24027:2021 | Bias in AI systems | Sources and measurement of bias |
:::

- Only 42001 is a certifiable management-system standard; it shares 27001's clause structure, so an existing ISMS extends to cover AI

### NIST AI Risk Management Framework
- ==National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)==: NIST AI 100-1, version 1.0, January 2023
  - Voluntary; NIST is revising it under the 2025 White House AI Action Plan, and 1.0 remains the published version

| Function | Does |
|---|---|
| ==Govern== | Cross-cutting culture, policies, accountability, third-party risk; applies to all others |
| ==Map== | Establish context: purpose, users, impacts, risks |
| ==Measure== | Test, evaluate, and track the mapped risks |
| ==Manage== | Prioritize, treat, respond to, and recover from risks |

- Seven trustworthy characteristics: valid and reliable; safe; secure and resilient; accountable and transparent; explainable and interpretable; privacy-enhanced; fair with harmful bias managed
- The Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1, July 2024) applies the RMF to generative AI
  - Lists 12 risks, including confabulation, data privacy, information security, information integrity, intellectual property, harmful bias or homogenization, and value chain and component integration; suggests actions per RMF function

:::exam
- EU AI Act tiers by example: social scoring is prohibited, CV screening is high risk, a support chatbot is limited risk, a spam filter is minimal.
- The builder is the provider; the user is the deployer. Rebranding or substantially modifying a high-risk system makes a deployer a provider.
- ISO/IEC 42001 is certifiable; the NIST AI RMF (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage) is voluntary.
:::
