### AI-generated content (deepfake)
- ==AI-generated content==: text, images, audio, or video produced by a model
  - A ==deepfake== is synthetic media that shows a real person saying or doing something they never did
- Forms an analyst will meet
  - ==Voice clone==: a few seconds to a minute of clean audio (a webinar, a voicemail greeting) is enough for a usable copy
  - ==Face swap== and lip sync on live video calls
  - Synthetic photos and forged ID documents for account opening and remote hiring
  - Fluent text in any language, any writing style, at any volume
- Why it matters for security
  - Defeats "I recognized the voice" and "I saw them on camera" as proof of identity
  - Cost drops to almost nothing, so attackers can target thousands of employees instead of a few executives
  - Ready-made fake media is sold as a service to criminals with no technical skill

### Adversarial networks
- A ==generative adversarial network (GAN)== trains two models against each other
  - ==Generator==: makes fakes from random noise
  - ==Discriminator==: judges real versus fake
  - Each round, the generator learns from what got caught; training ends when the discriminator is right about half the time
- Introduced by Goodfellow and colleagues in 2014; StyleGAN made photorealistic faces of people who do not exist
- Most current image and video tools use ==diffusion models==, but the adversarial idea still drives the problem
  - Any published deepfake detector can serve as the discriminator for the next generator
  - Detection tools fall behind each new generation, so do not rely on spotting artifacts

:::figure 3.2-gan-loop.svg | The generator improves until the discriminator can no longer tell. A public deepfake detector can be used the same way.

- Visible artifacts (odd blinking, warped earrings, flat audio) help today and fade tomorrow
  - Train people on the process (verify through another channel) rather than only on the artifacts

### Impersonation and social engineering
- ==Impersonation==: posing as a trusted person or brand
  - Executive voice or video asking for a payment, a gift card purchase, or a password reset
  - Fake IT help desk calling an employee; fake employee calling the help desk
  - In 2024 an Arup employee in Hong Kong joined a video call in which the CFO and colleagues were all deepfakes, then made 15 transfers totaling about HK$200 million (US$25 million)
  - In 2025 the FBI warned of AI voice messages impersonating senior US officials
- ==Social engineering== at scale
  - LLMs write phishing with no spelling mistakes, in the target's language, referencing real projects
  - Chatbots keep a conversation going with many victims at once (romance and investment scams)
  - The classic tells (bad grammar, generic greeting) no longer apply
- Countermeasures that work regardless of how good the fake is
  - ==Verification callback==: hang up and call back on a number from the directory, never one given in the message
  - Out-of-band confirmation in a second channel (chat, ticket) for any payment or access change
  - Dual approval and waiting periods for wire transfers and vendor bank changes
  - Pre-agreed code words for executives and their assistants
  - ==Phishing-resistant MFA== (FIDO2 passkeys): a convincing story still cannot replay a hardware-bound login
  - Help desk resets require identity proofing: manager approval or an ID check

### Reconnaissance and automated data correlation
- ==Reconnaissance==: gathering facts about the target before the attack
  - AI agents read LinkedIn, company sites, GitHub, conference talks, and job postings in minutes
  - Output: org chart, who approves payments, which vendors and tools are in use, writing samples to imitate
- ==Automated data correlation==: joining scattered data into one profile
  - Breach dumps plus social media plus public records
  - Pieces that are harmless alone (a username here, a birthday there) together answer security questions or seed a believable pretext
  - Also used to pick targets: who has finance access and just posted about travel
- Defenses
  - Reduce exposure: limit org charts and tool lists on public pages; review what job postings reveal
  - Monitor for leaked credentials and employee data; reset on discovery
  - Teach staff that a caller who knows internal details is not proven to be internal

### Misinformation, disinformation, and obfuscation
- ==Misinformation==: false or misleading content shared without intent to deceive
  - Includes a model's own confident errors repeated by a user (`LLM09:2025 Misinformation`)
- ==Disinformation==: false content created and spread on purpose
  - Fake executive statements to move a stock price; fake breach announcements; election influence operations
  - AI supplies fake personas, comment floods, and translated content cheaply
  - The ==liar's dividend==: real evidence gets dismissed as "probably a deepfake"
- ==Obfuscation==: hiding malicious intent from filters and analysts
  - AI paraphrases a lure so no signature matches (see 3.1); swaps in look-alike characters
  - Puts the text inside an image, a QR code, or a PDF attachment
  - Rewrites scripts so they look different each time
  - Counter with detection based on behavior and intent: the link's destination, the sender's history, what the process does
- Provenance controls
  - ==Content Credentials== (C2PA): a cryptographically signed record of who made a file and how it was edited
  - Invisible watermarks such as Google SynthID mark AI output
  - Both can be stripped by a screenshot or re-encode: a missing credential proves nothing; a valid one is strong evidence
  - The EU AI Act (Article 50) requires deepfakes to be disclosed as AI-generated
- Communications plan
  - Publish official statements in known channels; have a process to report and take down fakes quickly

:::wide
| Attack | What the defender sees | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Deepfake executive on a call | urgent payment, secrecy, unusual channel | verification callback, dual approval |
| Voice clone to help desk | caller knows details, wants MFA reset | identity proofing, manager approval |
| AI-written phishing | fluent, personal, references real work | phishing-resistant MFA, link analysis, reporting button |
| Automated reconnaissance | scraping, spikes in profile views | reduce public data, leaked-credential monitoring |
| Disinformation | fake statement spreading on social media | official channels, Content Credentials on real media |
| Obfuscated lure | text in images, look-alike characters | behavior and intent-based filtering |
:::

### Training that matches the threat
- Update ==security awareness training==
  - Show real deepfake audio and video; run ==vishing== and video-call simulations alongside email tests
  - Teach the pressure signs: urgency, secrecy, authority, a new payment method
- Make verification normal and blameless
  - Executives say in advance: "I will never ask you to skip the callback"
  - Reward reporting, including false alarms
- Tabletop a deepfake scenario with finance, HR, legal, and communications

:::exam
- "The voice sounded exactly like the CFO" means the control must not depend on recognition: the answer is a verification callback through a known number, or dual approval.
- Misinformation lacks intent; disinformation is deliberate. A GAN is a generator and a discriminator trained against each other.
- Automated data correlation joins separate public or breached data into one profile; it supports reconnaissance and pretexting, not direct system access.
:::
